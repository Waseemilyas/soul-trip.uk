## The destination

The trustworthy public face of SoulTrip Travel and Tours: the page a UK
family checks before entrusting a Hajj or Umrah journey to a young company,
and a business traveller checks before a Saudi trip. It presents what the
company genuinely offers — spiritual journeys, business travel, Saudi
opportunities — warmly and plainly, and turns a visitor's trust into an
enquiry that reliably reaches the company. For a company this young, the
site carries the burden of first impressions: it must look and behave like
a firm that will still exist next year.

## What it feels like when it works

- A family researching Umrah finds clear, honest pages and enquires with
  confidence.
- Every enquiry arrives; none vanish into a broken form or an unwatched
  inbox.
- The site is fast, works on any phone, and never breaks silently — every
  change is verified live, because downtime on a trust-selling site costs
  trust.

## Non-negotiables

- **Nothing on it is untrue.** Claims about licensing, partnerships and
  experience match reality — a travel company selling pilgrimage journeys
  cannot afford one caught exaggeration.
- **The enquiry path is the product.** Whatever else changes, contact must
  work, verifiably.
- **It stays worthy of the subject.** Hajj and Umrah pages are handled with
  the dignity the journey deserves, never as package-holiday inventory.

## Not this

- Not a booking engine. No payments, availability or itinerary management —
  enquiry and conversation, not checkout.
- Not a content treadmill: no blog for its own sake, no SEO filler diluting
  a trust-first site.
- Not a platform for a second brand or resold offers; one company, its own
  services.

## Judging a change against this vision

- Would it make a cautious family trust the company more, or smell
  marketing?
- Does the enquiry path still provably work after the change?
- Is it still a brochure a young firm can keep true — or has it grown
  operations it cannot honour?
